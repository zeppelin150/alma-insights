## 3. WHAT'S GETTING WORSE

Identify 3-5 trends that are deteriorating. Prioritize by urgency (how quickly the trend is worsening) and impact (how many customers are affected).

For each deteriorating trend:

### [Rank]. [Trend Name]
- **Trend**: [clear description of what is worsening]
- **Velocity**: How fast is this deteriorating? (e.g., "X% increase over N weeks", "new issue first seen in [period]")
- **Statistical Backing**: [reference theta flags, rising terms, incident flags, CUSUM alerts from statistical context]
- **Qualitative Evidence**: [reference specific ticket themes, sentiment shifts, new complaint types from TRC analyses]
- **Affected TRCs**: [list TRC codes]
- **Risk Assessment**: What happens if this continues unchecked for 30/60/90 days?
- **Early Warning Signals**: What leading indicators should leadership monitor?

Types of deterioration to look for:
1. **Rising friction**: Known issues getting worse (increasing prevalence, severity, or scope)
2. **New friction**: Issues that didn't exist in prior periods (novel complaints, new failure modes)
3. **Spreading friction**: Issues jumping from 1-2 TRCs to 3+ TRCs (localized → systemic)
4. **Sentiment deterioration**: Customer frustration intensifying even if volume is stable
5. **Statistical anomalies**: Theta flags, CUSUM alerts, incident flags that indicate process shifts

RULES:
- Focus on CHANGE and NOVELTY — stable patterns belong in Section 2 (friction points), not here
- Every claim must reference either ticket data or statistical signals
- Cross-reference NLP baseline data to distinguish genuinely new issues from pre-existing ones
- Cross-reference the accumulator context if provided — if the accumulator flagged a worsening trend, validate it against statistical evidence
- Quantify the rate of change, not just the current state
- Use plain business language — this report is for operations leadership

- Do NOT invent data or extrapolate beyond the evidence
- Flag any "black swan" patterns — low frequency but potentially high impact
