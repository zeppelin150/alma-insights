You are grouping canonical issue clusters into higher-level CONCEPTS for a
healthcare support ticket analytics product.

Each cluster below describes a recurring customer friction pattern. Your job
is to partition the clusters into concepts, where a concept is a root
business issue that may manifest across multiple surface-level clusters
(different wording, slightly different root causes, different TRCs).

Each input cluster has:
  cluster_id — stable identifier (echo it exactly in your output)
  canonical_label — the human-readable label Phase 5 produced
  trc — operational category the cluster belongs to
  member_count — how many tickets are in the cluster
  representative_snippets — up to 3 short ticket excerpts
  top_key_phrases — aggregated phrases across cluster members

Hard constraints (failing any means your output is rejected):
1. Every cluster_id MUST appear in exactly one concept.
2. A concept MUST contain at least one cluster. Two clusters in a concept
   should share a SPECIFIC root business issue — not just a broad theme
   like "billing" or "portal". "Disputed invoice amount" and "incorrect
   cancellation fee" are DIFFERENT concepts even though both are billing.
3. If a cluster is genuinely standalone, put it alone in its own concept.
4. concept_label: 3-6 words, noun phrase, sentence case, no trailing
   punctuation, no PII.
5. concept_description: one sentence (≤ 20 words), starts with a noun.
6. Do NOT merge clusters that describe opposite intents (e.g. "refund
   requested" vs "refund already issued"), even if wording overlaps.
7. Prefer MORE, specific concepts over fewer broad ones. It is OK to
   produce 10-15 concepts from 20 clusters. Users filter by concept to
   find root causes — "Billing disputes" is too coarse to act on;
   "Duplicate invoice billing" and "Incorrect cancellation fee" are
   actionable. Only merge clusters when their root cause is IDENTICAL,
   not when they share a category.
8. Two clusters belong in the same concept only if a reader, seeing the
   labels side-by-side, would say "these describe the same underlying
   problem" — not just "these are about similar systems".

Output a single JSON object with this schema (no markdown, no prose
outside the JSON):

{
  "concepts": [
    {
      "concept_id": "<slug — lowercase, hyphen-separated, ≤ 40 chars>",
      "concept_label": "<Human label, 3-6 words>",
      "concept_description": "<≤ 20 words>",
      "cluster_ids": ["<cluster_id>", "<cluster_id>", ...],
      "rationale": "<one sentence on why these cluster together>",
      "confidence": 0.0-1.0
    },
    ...
  ]
}

Input clusters:
{clusters_json}
